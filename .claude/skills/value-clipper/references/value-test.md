# What counts as a value segment

## The one test (every content type)

**Would Thomas's audience genuinely benefit from watching this?** That audience is
ambitious students applying to top masters programmes (MiM, MBA, MiF), business-school
applicants, and banking / consulting hopefuls. A segment benefits them when they come away
with one of these:
- a better understanding of the application process,
- a truer picture of what pursuing a competitive career is actually like,
- what it takes to be someone who is trying to do big things.

If the honest answer is "not really", it isn't a clip, however good it sounds.

A segment qualifies by being one of these:

1. **Insider insight or a story** that only Thomas (or the mentor) would know, the kind of
   thing you'd only learn by talking to him. Admissions-committee behaviour, what actually
   got someone in, what went wrong for a real applicant, how a bank really hires.
2. **A super valuable lesson**: a principle that changes how they apply or prepare.
3. **Highly tactical information**: something they can do today (how to format X, when to
   apply, what to say in an interview, which test to take).
4. **A common worry or objection plus the answer that resolves it** ("I'm from a
   non-target", "I'll apply next year", "I can't afford it").

## Per content type

### CV reviews
- Value = **concrete feedback on the CV**: what's wrong, why it matters, the fix, and the
  score. Contact-detail fixes, formatting, quantifying, the "go abroad early" type of
  experience advice, the score and how to raise it.
- **Not** value: the reviewer reading the CV out loud ("Education, EDHEC, BBA, Yonsei,
  top 3% dean's list"), generic praise, and career or market tangents ("escape France",
  "the applicant pool is saturated"). The CV 08 clip was rejected as "a regurgitation of
  the CV" because half of it was the read-aloud. Tangents can be a separate clip only if
  they pass the general test on their own.
- Usually one clip per CV: the 3-4 strongest feedback points.

### GMAT sessions / Q&A
- Value = **one clear question plus a clear, usable answer.** The student's question is
  often the hook ("oh, there's a good question, cool, this is a clip"), so keep it.
- Not value: logistics, "can you hear me", a question the answer never actually resolves.

### Consultation calls
- Value = any of the four kinds above. Common worries and objections are a good place to
  start, but anything Thomas's audience would only hear by talking to him qualifies:
  insider insight, a story, a lesson, tactical advice.
- Not value: pricing negotiation, scheduling, the student's personal details.

### Podcasts / interviews
- Value = **a super valuable lesson, a story, or highly tactical information.**
- Not value: small talk, intros, the host's setup questions on their own, anything that
  needs the previous ten minutes to make sense.

## Presenting the list (Level 1 output)

Rank by value, strongest first. For each candidate:

```
### 1. <the main point, in one line>
**Type:** insight/story | lesson | tactical | worry + answer
**Where:** 00:12:04 -> 00:13:10 (66s raw)   **Speaker(s):** <who, or "not labelled: confirm">
> <verbatim key lines from the transcript, cleaned of filler only>
**Why it's valuable:** <one sentence on what the viewer gets>
**Privacy:** <student identifiable? name / school / face on screen -> needs blur + consent>
```

Then list near-misses separately with the specific weakness ("good story, no payoff").
Never quietly promote one. Wait for approval before cutting anything.

## Privacy flags (students)

Keep a student's question or story when it's the hook, but flag every segment where a
student is identifiable: their name said aloud, their school or employer named together
with personal details, their face on screen. The edit blurs or anonymises; consent is
checked before posting (CV Review 03 was taken down at the owner's request). For
consultation calls, assume no consent unless the user says otherwise.
